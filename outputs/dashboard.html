The Territory’s Population Equation

Population change has exactly three parts. For the Northern Territory one of them has been negative for 18 straight quarters, and the Territory grows anyway. This tracks which part makes up the difference, and how steady that part is.

90.4%
of the Territory’s population growth in the year to March 2026 came from net overseas migration. Natural increase added 1,969, interstate migration subtracted 1,589, and overseas migration added 3,585, for total growth of 3,965 on a population of 268,899.

Method

Reconciliation passed: components sum to 3,965, matching the change ABS publishes, difference 0.

Decomposition. Quarterly components for the Northern Territory from ABS table 310102, June 1981 to March 2026, 180 quarters. Population level from table 310104.

Validation before reporting. Natural increase plus net interstate plus net overseas migration must equal the change ABS publishes for the same period. The pipeline checks this first and refuses to report a pass that is not one.

Volatility. Coefficient of variation against the mean absolute value, because interstate migration is mostly negative and a signed mean would make the measure meaningless.

Smoothing. The chart plots a four-quarter rolling average. Quarterly figures are noisy and the shape is what matters; raw values are in the repository.

What the estimates cannot do. Net overseas migration uses the 12-of-16-month rule, so a person can change category without changing address. Interstate movement is derived from administrative address records, so people who rarely update them are counted late or not at all. Census undercount is higher in the Territory than nationally, especially in remote communities.

Scope. This work is descriptive. It characterises the structure of population change and the uncertainty attached to forecasting it. It makes no policy recommendation.
