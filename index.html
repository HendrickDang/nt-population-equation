The Territory’s Population Equation

Population change has exactly three parts. In the Northern Territory one of them has been negative for 18 straight quarters, and the population grows anyway. This page shows which part makes up the difference, and how steady that part is.

Limitations
  • Stated intentions, not observed behaviour. Nobody followed up to see who actually stayed.
  • The report publishes no subgroup sample sizes, so the number of people behind each bar is unknown.
  • Self-selected online sample. The report notes it under-represents people aged 18 to 34 and over 75.
  • Birthplace and home ownership are each a single-variable split, so the groups overlap and the two findings cannot be separated from each other.
  • An association with intending to stay is not evidence that home ownership causes retention. The relationship could run either way.

Method, limitations and scope

Decomposition. Quarterly components for the Northern Territory from ABS table 310102, June 1981 to March 2026, 180 quarters. Population level from table 310104.

Validation before reporting. Natural increase plus net interstate plus net overseas migration must equal the change the ABS publishes separately for the same period. The pipeline checks this first and writes nothing at all if it fails.

Volatility. Coefficient of variation against the mean absolute value, because interstate migration is mostly negative and a signed mean would make the measure meaningless.

Smoothing. The chart plots a four-quarter rolling average. Quarterly figures are noisy and the shape is what matters; raw values are in the repository.

A break in the series. The ABS moved net overseas migration to the 12-of-16-months rule from September 2006, so levels either side are not comparable. The volatility ranking is unchanged on the full series, on the period since the change, and on the period before it.

What the estimates cannot do. A person can change category under the 12-of-16-month rule without changing address. Interstate movement comes from administrative address records, so people who rarely update them are counted late or not at all. Census undercount is higher in the Territory than nationally, especially in remote communities.

Scope. This work is descriptive. It characterises the structure of population change and the uncertainty attached to forecasting it. It makes no policy recommendation.
